Why to object

Pick the points that matter to you and put them in your own words — a personal objection carries far more weight than a copied template.

  1. Disproportionate scale — Ansley Common is a Category 3 Local Service Centre. Policy says windfall development should normally be no more than 10 units. Allocating 850+ homes is wildly out of proportion.
  2. Loss of countryside — Open countryside separating Ansley Common from Nuneaton, Hartshill and Ansley Village. Development risks merging separate communities.
  3. Highways & traffic — Coleshill Road and surrounding lanes are already under pressure. Road infrastructure isn't funded or guaranteed.
  4. Strained infrastructure — GP surgeries, dentists and school places are already stretched with no evidence they can absorb 850+ additional homes.
  5. Flood risk — Site-specific flood, surface water and foul sewage assessments should be done before allocation.
  6. Agricultural land & wildlife — Productive farmland and green space. Significant loss of habitat, hedgerows and biodiversity.
  7. Village character — Ansley Common's rural character would be permanently changed by development of this size.
  8. Construction disruption — Years of construction traffic, noise, dust and disruption on rural roads not designed for it.
  9. Housing numbers contested — The Council cannot demonstrate a five-year housing land supply, and realistic delivery at H10 is disputed.
  10. Cumulative impact — H9, H10 and Hartshill together represent one of the largest concentrations of new housing in the Borough. Impact must be assessed as a whole.
Keep it factual. Planning objections are weighed on evidence and policy grounds. Specific points about traffic, infrastructure, flood risk and scale carry far more weight.
